## When you need a commercial license

You need a commercial license if you intend to:

- **Bundle claude-dejavu into a commercial product** that you sell or
  distribute to third parties.
- **Host claude-dejavu as a managed service** offered to third parties.
- **Build a product that competes with our commercial offering** (e.g. a
  managed grounding service, a hallucination-prevention dashboard, an
  alternative paid tier).
- **Modify and resell** claude-dejavu under another name or as part of
  another tool.
- **Avoid the FSL terms** for legal-team reasons (some corporate legal
  policies prefer a traditional commercial license over a source-available
  fair-source license — that's a perfectly valid reason to talk to us).

You **do not** need a commercial license to:

- Use claude-dejavu on your own codebase, your team's codebase, or your
  employer's codebase, internally.
- Use it as part of consulting or professional services delivered to a
  client (provided the client is also using it under FSL or a commercial
  license).
- Run it in your CI, on developer machines, on internal servers.
- Modify it for internal use without redistributing.
- Use it in research or teaching.
- Contribute back to the upstream project.

## How commercial licensing works

We're a small team. We're not interested in extracting maximum revenue
from every contact, and we're not running a license-compliance lawsuit
business. We want a fair share of the upside from companies that build
real revenue on top of our work.

### Pricing approach

Pricing is set per deal and depends on:

- **Use case** — internal vs. embedded vs. resold vs. SaaS-hosted
- **Company size** — number of employees and/or revenue tier
- **Scope** — single product, multiple products, full enterprise
- **Term** — annual, multi-year, perpetual

We typically structure deals as one of:

| Structure | When it fits |
|-----------|--------------|
| Flat annual fee | Internal corporate use that triggers FSL but isn't competitive |
| Per-seat license | Bundling into a developer-facing product |
| Per-deployment / per-product | OEM bundling |
| Revenue share | Strategic partnerships |
| Enterprise master agreement | Multi-year, multi-product |

### What you get with a commercial license

- A signed commercial license agreement that supersedes FSL for your use
- Removal of the Competing Use restriction within the agreed scope
- Optional: priority support, a direct line to maintainers, early access
  to new domain packs and features
- Optional: indemnification, warranty terms suitable for enterprise
  procurement

### What we don't ask for

- No NDA upfront — we'll talk about your use case first
- No mandatory implementation services
- No multi-year minimums for first-time deals
- No exclusivity demands
